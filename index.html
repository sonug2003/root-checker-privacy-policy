3. How the App Works

Root Checker performs local device checks to determine if your Android device has root access. Here's what happens:

  • Checks for su binary files in common system locations
  • Looks for known root management applications
  • Examines system build properties
  • Tests system partition write access
  • Checks for root-related paths and files

All these checks are performed locally on your device. No information is sent anywhere.

4.4 Understanding SDK Auto-Merged Permissions

What are "Auto-Merged" Permissions?

When you use third-party SDKs (like Google AdMob, Firebase, or Play Services), these libraries may declare their own permissions in their manifest files. During the build process, Android automatically "merges" these permissions into your app's final manifest. This is standard practice for all Android apps using advertising or analytics SDKs.

Why does this matter?

  • These permissions are not manually added by us but are required by Google's SDKs to function properly
  • Most are related to the Android Privacy Sandbox (Google's privacy-preserving advertising framework)
  • They enable privacy-first advertising without cross-app tracking

Your Control Over These Permissions:

  • Advertising ID: Reset or delete in Android Settings → Google → Ads
  • Privacy Sandbox: Manage in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads → Ad privacy
  • Personalized Ads: Opt-out via our in-app Privacy Settings or device settings
